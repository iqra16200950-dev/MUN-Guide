Committee Flow

A complete timeline of how a MUN (specifically UNA-USA rules of procedure) committee session works, so you don't have to spend time learning random stuff on the side.

1

Roll Call (about 5 minutes)

The chair calls each delegate's portfolio name. Delegates respond with "Present" or "Present and voting."

If you reply "Present," you can choose:

  • Yes = vote for the resolution
  • No = vote against the resolution
  • Abstain = no vote

If you reply "Present and voting," you can only choose:

  • Yes = vote for the resolution
  • No = vote against the resolution
2

GSL — General Speakers List (30–90 seconds per delegate)

The General Speakers List is the main formal opening session. It allows delegates to present their country's views on the agenda. Each delegate gets 30, 60, or 90 seconds depending on the motion raised. This is an important segment.

The GSL is usually non-exhaustive, meaning it does not end once everyone speaks. If it does end, the session may be suspended, which is uncommon. The chair may continue at their discretion.

Motion: The delegate of [portfolio name] raises a motion to establish the General Speakers List with [x] seconds per speaker and [x] speakers this round.
3

Moderated Caucus

A moderated caucus is a formal debate on a specific sub-topic of the agenda. There is usually a speakers list, and delegates may write substantive chits if they have not yet spoken.

In many conferences, especially three-day events, at least two moderated caucuses are held. Topics are often discussed in advance on official forums.

Motion: "The delegate of [portfolio] raises a motion to move into a Moderated Caucus on the topic [topic] for [x] minutes, allotting [x] seconds per speaker."
4

Unmoderated Caucus

An unmoderated caucus is less formal time to work with other delegates, write resolutions, form blocs, and negotiate. Use this time to build alliances and prepare your documents.

Motion: "The delegate of [portfolio] raises a motion to move into an unmoderated caucus for [x] minutes."
5

Papers & Draft Resolutions

After preparatory debate, delegates start working on documents. These documents may be marked, and there may be a limit on how many additional papers you can submit. The executive board will share deadlines and submission rules.

During this phase, delegates present working papers, press releases, MOUs, and other documents. Draft resolutions are also presented, though voting may not happen immediately.

6

Amendments

Delegates propose changes to draft resolutions. Friendly amendments are agreed by sponsors, while unfriendly amendments are debated and voted on.

7

Voting Procedure

Committees vote on amendments and then the final resolution. A simple majority is usually required for a resolution to pass. If your resolution is tabled, congratulations — your committee succeeded. If no resolution passes, the committee fails.

8

Closing and Awards

Chairs announce awards during the closing ceremony: Best Delegate, Outstanding Delegate, Honourable Mention, Special Mention, Verbal Mention, and more.

Essential Motions & Points

Point of Personal Privilege

During any discussion, a delegate can raise a point of personal privilege for issues such as being unable to hear the speaker. These points relate to comfort, safety, and wellbeing.

Example: [raise placard and wait to be recognized] Chairperson, Point of personal privilege, the current speaker is inaudible.

Point of Order (POO)

A point of order is raised when a delegate violates procedure, makes an illogical argument, or uses incorrect terminology. These points can earn high marks when used correctly.

Types of Points of Order:

  • Logical Fallacy: Raised when a delegate's argument is logically unsound or self-contradictory. The format is: Chairperson, Point of Order, Logical Fallacy, the delegate said "[verbatim]", however this is illogical because [reason].
  • Factual Inaccuracy: Raised when a delegate states false information. Example: Delegate A says, "This was written in the Rome Statute." Delegate B raises a point of order: Chairperson, Point of Order, Factual Inaccuracy, the delegate said "Roman Statute," but the correct document is the Rome Statute.

Point of Information (POI)

If a speaker yields time to POIs, delegates may ask questions about the speech. Delegates are evaluated on both the question and the answer.

How to raise a POI: [raise placard and wait to be recognized] Chairperson, Point of Information, [question].

Example: How can the delegate claim to support green energy when their policy included commissioning coal power plants?

In UNA-USA procedure, POIs are usually accepted during the GSL, either verbally or via chit. POOs are generally only raised during moderated caucus.

Point of Parliamentary Procedure / Inquiry (POPP / POPI)

This point is used to ask for clarification on procedural issues. It should not interrupt a speaker and is not usually marked.

Yielding Time

If a delegate finishes their speech early, they can yield their remaining time. The options are:

  • Yield to another delegate
  • Yield to questions (POIs)
  • Yield to the Chair

Right of Reply (ROR)

When a delegate is personally attacked or insulted, they may request a right of reply. The chair may choose whether to read it aloud.

Appeal to the Chair’s Decision

This appeal is made when a delegate believes the chair made an incorrect ruling. The issue is written on paper and may be reviewed by the Deputy Secretary General.

Motion to Suspend Debate

This motion temporarily stops the current discussion and transitions to another procedure such as a moderated or unmoderated caucus.

Motion: The delegate of [portfolio] raises a motion to suspend debate and move into [procedure].

Motion to Table Debate

This motion ends discussion on the current topic and moves the committee to the next agenda item.

Motion: The delegate of [portfolio] raises a motion to table debate and move on to the next topic.

Closure of Debate

This motion ends discussion on the current topic and closes any amendments and papers.

Adjournment of the Meeting

This motion ends the committee session. If passed, the session is adjourned until the next scheduled meeting.
