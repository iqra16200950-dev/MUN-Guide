Committee Flow

A complete timeline of how a MUN (specifically UNA-USA rules of procedure) committee session works, so you don't have to suffer understanding random stuff like I did <3

1

Roll Call (like 5 mins max)

The chair calls out each delegates portfolio name . Delegates respond with "Present" or "Present and voting"

If you reply with "Present" this means you have three choices towards the voting of the resolution:

  • 1. You can vote for the resolution (Yes)
  • 2. You can vote against the resolution (No)
  • 3. You can abstain from voting (No vote at all)


    • If you choose "Present and voting" you only have two definite choices:
  • 1. You can vote for the resolution (Yes)
  • 2. You can vote against a resolution (No)
  • 3. You CANNOT abstain from voting (Must vote)

    • 2

    GSL-General Speakers List(30-90 seconds per delegate)

    The General Speakers List is the main formal opening speech, it allows delegates to address and present their views on the agenda.

    Each delegate gets 30/60/90 seconds (depends on the motion raised) to present their country's positions and views. This is a very important segment.

    The GSL is non-exhaustible, meaning it cannot conclude (all delegates do their GSL), if it does, the committee session may be suspended. This is very uncommon and won't happen most of the times. If it does, the chair may also continue under chair's discretion.

    It is very important to note that motions are raised for this (motions are formal requests for the beginning or closing of a session/segment)

    Motion: The delegate of [portfolio name] raises a motion to establish the General Speakers List with [x] seconds/time per speaker and [x] number of speakers this round"
    3

    Moderated Caucus

    The name is weird right? Anyways, moderated caucuses are sessions of intense formal debate on specific sub-topics related to the agenda. For this usually there is also a list and if by the end of conference you haven't given a speech you are given the permission to write substantive chits on the topics (same applies for GSL).

    In many conferences (especially 3 day conferences) at least 2 topics will be discussed via moderated caucuses. Most of the time the topics are discussed on official forums before the starting of the conference.

    Motion: "The delegate of [portfolio] raises a motion to move into a Moderated Caucus on the topic [topic] for the time period of [x] minutes , allotting [x] seconds per speaker"
    4

    Unmoderated Caucus

    This is the time where you chill and try to get some work done - including making alliances, interacting with other delegates and maybe even starting to write your resolution. This time can also be used for discussions with other delegates in terms of further procedures.

    I urge you to use this precious time to form alliances and maybe even get into blocs; or make one (Blocs are group of delegates with similar view and positions on the agenda, these groups then work together to write a resolution with the ideology of the bloc in mind).

    Motion:"The delegate of [portfolio] raises a motion to move into an unmoderated caucus for the time period of [x] minutes"
    5

    Papers & Draft Resolutions

    After all of this, it's time to start working on your documents. These written documents will be marked too, there may be a cap on the amount of how many additional documents you can write. Deadline and details of additional documents being accepted will be given prior by the executive board. Once the chair starts the voting procedure on additional documents, delegates introduce their working papers, press releases, MOUs and more (more details in the document section of the website) Then delegates get to vote on whether or not the additional document passes. During this time draft resolutions are also presented but voting most likely doesn't take place yet.

    6

    Amendments

    Delegates propose changes to draft resolutions. Friendly (sponsor agrees) vs unfriendly (debated)

    7

    Voting Procedure

    Buckle up, cause this is gonna get intense. Committees vote on all amendments, then final resolutions. Simple majority is required for a resolution to be tabled (to pass). If your resolution was tabled, CONGRATULATIONS YOU ARE ONE OF THE BIGGEST REASONS WHY YOUR COMMITTEE DIDN'T FAIL! If no resolutions get tabled, the committee fails.

    8

    Closing And Awards

    Chairs announce the awards during the closing ceremony: Best Delegate, Outstanding Delegate, Honourable Mention, Special Mention, Verbal Mention and more!

    Essential Motions & Points

    Point Of Personal Privilege

    During any discussion of any matter, a delegate can raise a point of personal privilege. This point is for personal matters - like for example during a GSL speech, the speaker was inaudible, here you have the right to raise a point of personal privilege and tell the chair about it. These points refer to the personal comfort, safety and wellbeing of the delegates.

    Example: [first raise your placards and wait until chair recognizes you] and then you say: Chairperson, Point of personal privilege, The current speaker is inaudible.

    These points will not be marked

    Point Of Order (POO)

    A point of order is raised when a delegate or committee member has made a false statement, violated rules, made illogical arguments or maybe even messed up the name of a reference.

    These points get marked HIGH, like for a delegate's point of order to have stood completely is like getting 10 steps closer to the top 3.

    Tip: Do not shy away from raising these points, don't be scared.

    There are 2 types of points of orders:

  • 1. Logical Fallacy: This is raised when a delegate's argument is logically unsound or contradicts itself. A logical fallacy is a flaw in the reasoning structure of an argument, making the conclusion invalid. So basically flawed reasoning, not flawed facts.

    How to raise: [raise placard, once chair recognizes start speaking]
    Chairperson, Point of Order, Logical Fallacy, The delegate mentioned "Verbatim (quote the delegate's flawed statements)", however this is illogical as [how it's flawed].

    It is very important for the verbatim to be correct, else your point will definitely not stand. If you are confused, first raise a point of personal privilege for repetition of the certain part of the speech, then raise point of order.

  • 2. Factual Inaccuracy: This is when a delegate makes a statement where the facts are wrong. Getting the name of a document counts as factual inaccuracy.

    Example: (delegate A) says: This has been written in the Rome Statute.

    (Delegate B) raises POO; Chairperson, Point of Order, Factual Inaccuracy, the delegate mentioned "Roman Statute", however there is no such document. The document that states this is the Rome Statute.

    The formatting of this is the same to raising a logical fallacy POO.

    • Point Of Information (POI)

    After a delegate concludes their speech, and if the delegate yields their time to POIs, a question regarding the speech may be asked. Delegates are marked for the question and the replies. If the answer did not satisfy, you can plead for a follow-up.

    How to raise a POI: [raise placard, wait until recognized by chair] Chairperson, Point of Information, [question]

    Tips:

  • Try to quote or catch a hypocritical statement, combine it with a contradictory past action, deliver your question with confidence and make sure to ask questions that might leave them speechless.

    • Example: how can the delegate claim to embrace green energy when their policy for the past five years has involved commissioning coal power plants?

    In UNA-USA ROP, POIs are only accepted during the GSL, both verbally and via chit. Once chair announces floor is open for chits. One can only raise POOs during moderated caucus

    Point of Parliamentary Procedure/inquiry (POPP/POPI)

    This point is raised for clarification on procedural confusions and issues. This should never interrupt a speaker and should be asked when no discussion is in progress. You will not be marked for this.

    Yeilding Time

    Though this isn't a motion or point, it is an important part of the GSL. The delegate who concludes their GSL speech before the time limit is reached can yield (give) their time.

    There are 3 ways:

  • 1. Yield to another delegate = remaining time will be given to them
  • 2. Yield to questions (POIs) = Delegates can ask questions relating to the speech. Questions & answer must remain limited to the time remaining.
  • 3. Yield to Chair = If delegates don't want the above options, they yield to the chair.

    • Right Of Reply(ROR)

    When personal attacks and insults have been made by a delegate,Right of replies can be submitted to the chair ,this will most likely be marked. The chair may or may not choose to read out the ROR .

    RORs spice up the conference and honestly the drama is soo fun lol.

    Appeal to the chairs Decision

    An appeal is made when a delegate believes that the chair made an incorrect ruling, this appeal will be written on paper.And further deciisions will be made by the Deputy Secretary General after hearing both sides.

    Motion to suspend debate(motion for a caucus)

    This motion is raised to temporarily halt the discussion at hand and move on to either a moderated caucus or an unmod session.
    Before raising motion for unmod or mod , you must first raise a motion to suspend debate .

    Motion: The delegate of [portoflio] raises a motion to suspend debate and move into [different procedure].

    Motion to Table Debate

    This motion is raised to end the discussion on the current topic and move on to the next one. This is usually raised when the chair thinks that the discussion has reached a dead end and further discussion won't be productive.

    Motion: The delegate of [portfolio] raises a motion to table debate and move on to the next topic.

    Closure of debate

    This motion is raised to end the discussion on the current topic ,concluding and closing any amendments and papers.

    Adjournment of the meeting

    This motion is raised to end the committee session. After adjournment,the committee will continue at its next meeting (if left). This motion once passed adjourns the session.
