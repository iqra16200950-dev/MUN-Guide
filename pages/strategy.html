Winning Strategy

Some cool tactics to get placement

Award Winner Criteria

25%

Research and knowledge

You need to have deep country policy knowledge, topic expertise, and accurate facts. You must have the ability to put forth strong arguments and answer questions clearly, especially in press conferences.

25%

Speeches

Speeches should be clear, persuasive, and well structured. Your research should be showcased through your speeches. Make sure there is a good balance of opinion and facts, and avoid using AI.

25%

Documents

Quality position paper, additional document, and resolution authorship will improve your overall score.

4 pillars of getting placements

Be active & significant

  • Be active in the committee.
  • Try to speak as much as you can. The more points you put across, the better.
  • Do not scream during your speeches; this can cost you marks.
  • Don't bring up insignificant discussions. Make sure your points are relevant to the agenda.

Build Your Bloc

  • Communicate with other delegates and build relationships. Use your unmoderated caucus time wisely.
  • Always try to write your own resolution. Being a sponsor earns recognition.
  • Try leading, but don't be bossy; show leadership through collaboration.

Control Debate

  • Showcase your research.
  • Yield to allies.
  • Try to answer as many POIs as you can — this shows your preparedness.

Perfect Document

  • Write world-breaking papers
  • Make smart amendments
  • Format your documents perfectly

Bloc Building Strategy

Day 1

Research All Countries

Know every delegate's likely position before the conference starts. Have organized documents and keep researching before the conference.

Unmoderated Caucus

Form/Create core groups

Get 5–7 committed signatories and sponsors as soon as possible. Build good relationships so they support you during voting.

1

Not researching country policy

2

Speaking too soft,using irrelevant information and speaking for too long

3

Being too shy and making no alliances

4

Poor position paper

5

Being inactive and not known in the committee
