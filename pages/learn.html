Learn MUN Basics

Everything you need to know before your first conference!

What is the United Nations?

The United Nations (UN) is an international organization founded in 1945 after World War II. Its purpose is to maintain international peace and security, develop friendly relations among nations, achieve international cooperation, and serve as a center for harmonizing the actions of nations. It supports accountability, transparency, and global development.

Key Facts

  • Founded in 1945 after World War II as the successor to the League of Nations
  • 193 member states
  • Headquarters in New York City
  • 6 official languages: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish
  • Main organs: General Assembly (UNGA), Security Council (UNSC), Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC), Trusteeship Council, International Court of Justice (ICJ), Secretariat
  • These organs include subsidiary bodies, commissions, departments, specialized agencies, and programmes that support research, training, and global cooperation.

What is Model United Nations?

Educational Simulation

MUN simulates the UN (and sometimes fictional committees) where students become "delegates" representing countries, people, or organizations. We research, write position papers, debate solutions, give speeches, and vote on draft resolutions — just like real diplomats. You argue professionally and stand up for your portfolio even if you don’t personally agree with it.

Competitive Activity

Conference awards such as Best Delegate, Outstanding Delegate, and Honourable Mention are based on research, speeches, diplomacy, and leadership. Being too aggressive can hurt your chances, so stay confident but respectful.

Key Roles in MUN

UN

Delegate

You!
Represent a portfolio. Research its policies and history, speak for it, negotiate alliances, write resolutions, and vote.

  • Research your assigned country thoroughly
  • Write strong documents such as position papers and working papers
  • Deliver clear and persuasive speeches
  • Build alliances with other delegates
🪑

Chair(s)

Chairs lead the committee, moderate debate, keep time, enforce rules, and decide awards. They should be experienced, impartial, and authoritative without being harsh.

  • Control the speakers list
  • Manage caucuses and motions
  • Recognize speakers fairly
📋

Rapporteur, Co-chair, Vice Chairperson, Moderator

These assistants support the chair by taking notes, managing paperwork, helping with voting, and maintaining order.

  • Manage records and documentation
  • Support the resolution process
  • Assist with voting procedures and decorum
