Position Paper

What is a Position Paper? A position paper is a document that outlines a country's stance on a particular issue or topic. It serves as a foundation for the country's arguments and proposals during committee sessions. basically a 1-2 page document summarizing your portfolios stance, position and policies before the conference.It is required by most conferences ,and one must uphold the same ideology they had in the position paper during the conference.

Basic Formatting & Structure

Heading

  • Committee Name
  • Topic/Agenda
  • Country Name
  • Delegate Name/school

Body (3 paragraphs)

  • Context ,state position , background (past actions)
  • Current policies
  • Solution,beliefs ,proposals and conclusion

  • Tip:Make sure to perfectly format it according to the instructions given by the executive board. As a chair i have seen so many papers that had good content but were marked down for fomatting errors.

Draft Resolution

Main goal: The draft resolution is a solution-based document passed by a committee via votes. All those debates, arguments and discussions were literally just done for this document. This is the end goal of it all.

This document also decides whether the committee failed or passed. It is usually very hard to write a draft resolution in 30 minutes. I have had to scrap many resolutions because of formatting issues, missed deadlines, and structure problems. The ones that pass and get tabled are usually the most thorough.

Structure

Preambulatory Clauses

The preambulatory clauses start with the committee name, and then clauses beginning with preambulatory verbs.

Every preambulatory clause ends with a comma (,). If any other punctuation is used, consider your draft resolution scraped.

Operative Clauses (Actions)

Operative clauses are the actionable parts of the resolution, detailing specific steps or measures to be taken. Please be very careful when writing these: if an operative clause is not within the committee mandate, your resolution will be considered wrong.

Every operative clause ends with a semicolon (;).

  • Formatting:
  • 1. Every preambulatory clause ends with a comma (,).
  • 2. Every operative clause ends with a semicolon (;).
  • 3. Every sub-clause to a resolution should end with a comma (,) until the final sub-clause of the main clause, which should end with a semicolon (;).
  • 4. Every main clause before starting a sub-clause should have a colon (:).
  • 5. Full stop at the end of the resolution.
  • Most of the time every preambulatory word is italicised, and every operative verb is bold and/or underlined.

    Please make sure to ask your chairs to share their expected formatting of the draft resolution.

Sample Draft Resolution

Name of resolution
Sponsors (Authors): United Kingdom, France, People's Republic of China
Signatories (Supporters/co-authors): (Many countries who signed up to support your draft resolution but are not the main authors)
Topic: Addressing Human Trafficking
XYZ Committee name,
 1. Alarmed by the 25 million victims of human trafficking worldwide,
 2. Recalling its resolution 67/190 on human trafficking,
 3. Recognizing the Palermo Protocol as the global standard,

 1. Urges all Member States to strengthen border controls;
 2. Calls upon developed nations to fund anti-trafficking programs in developing and underdeveloped nations;
 3. Requests the creation of a human trafficking database to track down and identify traffickers and victims.

Working Paper

Purpose : A working paper is a preliminary document that outlines the key issues and proposed solutions for discussion by the committee.Its and informal created during unmods.Most of these evolve into Drafts resolutions

                WORKING PAPER 1.3
Sponsors:Kingdom of Morocco, Republic of India, Federal Republic of Nigeria

Signatories:Brazil, Indonesia, Kenya, UAE, Egypt

Committee:UNGA Third Committee (SOCHUM)

Agenda:Strengthening International Cooperation to Combat Human Trafficking

THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY,

Alarmed by the continued rise of transnational human trafficking networks, particularly affecting women, children, refugees, and economically vulnerable populations,

Recognizing that poverty, armed conflict, forced displacement, corruption, and digital exploitation contribute significantly to trafficking operations,

Deeply concerned by the lack of rehabilitation systems for survivors and weak international enforcement mechanisms,

Reaffirming the principles established under the United Nations Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons,

Proposes:
1. Creation of an International Anti-Trafficking Intelligence Network (IATIN)
Encourages member states to share trafficking data through encrypted intergovernmental channels;
Recommends regional trafficking surveillance hubs under UN supervision;
2. Survivor Rehabilitation and Reintegration
Calls for trauma-informed rehabilitation centers;
Encourages vocational education and employment assistance programs for survivors;
3. Border and Cyber Monitoring
Recommends AI-assisted monitoring of suspicious trafficking activity online;
Urges stronger border cooperation in high-risk trafficking corridors;
4. Economic Prevention Measures
Encourages microfinance initiatives for vulnerable communities;
Supports educational campaigns targeting rural populations and migrant workers;
5. International Accountability
Calls upon nations to criminalize all forms of human trafficking under domestic law;
Encourages annual transparency reports regarding trafficking prosecutions and convictions.

Amendments

Friendly Amendments

Sponsors agree to change. No debate or voting takes place

                    Friendly Amendment to Clause 2 of Draft Resolution 6.7
                        Original:"Calls upon developed nations"
                        Amended:"Calls upon all capable nations"

Unfriendly Amendments

Sponsor most likely disagress, it is debated and voted upon

                    Amendment A1 to Resolution 1/23
                    Add new clause 5:"Condemns economic sanctions as counter-productive "

Additional Documents

Press Release

Public statement made by a Bloc/Nations on position

MoU (Memorandum of Understanding)

An informal agreement, alliance or even treaty between nations

Crisis Directives

Crisis committees only;Response to crisis updates, secret operations can be done via DIR letters

Treaties (eg: Multilateral Treaty)

A highly formal and legally binding agreement ,more biding and detailed than MoUs.
